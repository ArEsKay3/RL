# Chain G (MINF from scratch, no prefix cache): what changed at step 34 -> 35

Date: 2026-09-22. Data: SingleController metrics, dumps/rollouts (per-rollout summaries in analysis/rollouts), dumps/token_level (analysis/tokens rows).

## The event

Steps 35 and 36 contain 2-3x more **runaway reasoning turns**: a single `reasoning` (thinking) item of 100k-180k generated tokens
that fills the context window (reward 0, `agent_error_kind=context_window`). The typical rollout did not change.

All 38 (step 35) and 30 (step 36) runaway items are `reasoning` items. Median distinct-12-gram ratio in their last 60k chars is 0.02
(98 % repeated 12-grams); the median most-repeated sentence recurs 75x. Across the 38 step-35 texts the phrase "Given the time" appears

item of the rollout. Example loop sentence (dtolnay__paste-4, 180k tokens): "Given the time, I'll try to use `Span::call_site()` but with a
"fresh" span by using `Span::new(0, 0)` but that's not good."

* Chain F (MINF from vLLM step 10) saw the identical 32 instances at steps 34 and 35 (in_order sampler, 32/32 overlap): 0 runaways,
  0 truncations, longest single turn 42k / 19k tokens (chain G: 150k / 161k). dtolnay__paste-4: G 7 runaways and 5/16 solved; F 0 and 13/16 solved.
  sindresorhus__dot-prop-105: G 3 runaways, 14/16; F 0, 16/16.
* No resume boundary at 34->35 (segment 3880174 resumed from step_32 and trained 33-36); the two post-resume batches (33, 34) were normal, and the
  earlier resume at step_14 produced no spike at 15-17 either. Weight sync 4.6 s, grad_norm 0.030, lr unchanged, no engine faults.
* Main chain (MINF from scratch, original) shows no spike at 35 (trunc 2.3 %) - different weights.

## Why the trainer metrics moved

* Tokens from gen>100k rollouts: 15 % (34) -> 31 % (35) -> 26 % (36) of the batch, at mean logprob -0.026 vs -0.135..-0.148 for the rest.
  Token-weighted mean logprob of the non-runaway rollouts moved only -0.148 -> -0.135, so the entropy drop (0.130 -> 0.101) is mostly composition.
* advantages/mean -0.063 -> -0.216 (loss 0.063 -> 0.216): at step 35 the loops landed in mixed groups (dot-prop 14/16 solved -> adv -2.28 per loop,
  linopy -1.93, serverless-webpack -1.60, paste-4 -0.68), so 44 % of |adv|*tokens came from runaways (13 % at step 34, where most loops sat in
  all-fail groups with adv 0). advantages/max 3.61 -> 1.93 because no group had a lone success (chance).
* 17 of the 27 single-turn runaways at step 35 were in all-fail groups -> advantage exactly 0 -> no learning signal against them. The overlong
  penalty never fires: max_response_length 196,608 equals the context window, so a runaway only ever gets reward 0 from the harness.
* The `</think>` close-probability audit cannot see this mode: a truncated reasoning turn emits no `</think>` token. That is why earlier
  reports (mean length, geo-mean P) called chain G "no growth" while the tail was growing.

## Open points / options

1. The step-35 update carried the strongest anti-loop gradient of the run; its effect would first show in the batch generated by v35 (step 37),
   which was never produced (chain stopped at 36). Resuming from step_36 for a few steps would test whether the loop rate responds.
2. Recipe fixes: a truncation / overlong penalty so loops are negative even in all-fail groups (max_response_length below the window,
   stop_properly_penalty_coef, or a reward penalty on context_window kind); a per-turn generation cap (the runaway turns are single 150k-token
   generations); the sampling knobs are temperature 1.0, top_p 1.0, top_k null, no repetition penalty.
3. Root question remains why MINF sampling enters/persists in rumination loops more than vLLM on the same weights.

## What in the step-34 training batch could have caused it (v34 generated most step-35 loop tokens and all of batch 36)

Checked and ruled out: the 18 eval timeouts at step 34 (16 of them in one all-fail group, advantage 0; the other 2 minor), dataset/task-family
composition (24-28 SWE-rebench instances per step throughout), a resume artifact (batches 33 and 34 were both regenerated from step_32; the
step_14 resume produced no spike at 15-17), update statistics (grad_norm 0.034, lr 3e-6, sync 4.6 s), rollouts spanning the mid-generation
refit (all step-35 loops span v33->v34 because they are the slowest rollouts, but batch 36 has 30 loops with no spanning at all).

What stands out in batch 34 is one rollout, `elastic__curator-743` sample ..._g5: reward 1, advantage +1.60, 164 turns, 89,666 generated tokens,
of which a single reasoning item of 202k characters (~55k tokens) is a 5-sentence cycle repeated 146 times ("Let me try to run the file with
python3 using the -c flag ... / I think the issue is that the file has a hidden character ... / But the cat -v output shows nothing unusual / Let
me check the file with od -x again ..."). The item ends abruptly mid-cycle, the agent then runs pytest, continues normally for 55 more items,
fixes the bug and is rewarded. GRPO's sequence-level advantage reinforced the whole trajectory: this one rollout supplies 8.3 % of all
positive-advantage reasoning text at step 34 and 685 of the 1,315 "Let me try" occurrences in positive-advantage text; the advantage-weighted
pressure on "Let me try" is +4.0 at step 34 and negative at every other step in the window (32: -1.2, 33: -4.1, 35: -25.8). Step 34 also has
the weakest anti-rumination pressures of steps 32-35 ("Given the time" -10.9 vs -14.8 at 33 and -44.7 at 35; "But that" -4.0 vs -21.4 / -62.0;
"I think" -4.2 vs -18.1 / -64.5). Batch 36 (generated by v34) already contains 4 successful rollouts with 30k-123k-token reasoning turns
receiving positive advantage (0.21M adv*tokens vs 0.08M at step 34 and <=0.05M before): the reinforcement is escalating with the loop rate.

Why the objective cannot regulate this mode (token dumps, generation logprobs):

| rollout | tokens | plateau length | mean lp inside | mean (1-p) inside | mean (1-p) outside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| curator-743 step 34, adv +1.60, reward 1 | 89,666 | 55,000 | -0.0031 | 0.0018 | 0.055 |
| mdx-analyzer-410 step 34 runaway, reward 0 | 159,032 | 148,000 | -0.0004 | 0.0002 | 0.080 |
| paste-4 step 35 runaway, reward 0 | 180,124 | 149,000 | -0.0008 | 0.0004 | 0.071 |

Inside a loop the sampled token has p > 0.95 for 99.5-100 % of positions, so the policy-gradient weight (1-p) is 30-400x smaller than on normal
text: negative advantage on a runaway barely moves the loop body, and positive advantage on a recovered loop barely moves it either. The
gradient lands on the entry region (the ~30k tokens of failed debugging that precede the loop, where lp drops from -0.13 to 0) and only when
the advantage is non-zero, which for 10/15 loops at step 34 and 17/27 at step 35 it was not (all-fail groups). Net: the training signal is
nearly blind to loop bodies, weakly anti-loop on entries in mixed groups, and mildly pro-loop when a loop recovers (curator). The loop rate is
therefore governed by the sampling bias of engine and weights and drifts; a recovered loop that is reinforced (curator at 34) is the kind of
event that nudges it, and it is followed here by the largest burst of the run. With 32 prompts per step this is one run's evidence, not a
controlled result: the advantage-weighted long-turn mass at step s correlates +0.35 with loop count at s+2, against a lag-2 autocorrelation of
+0.41 for the loop count itself.

## Revision after a closer look at the curator-743 exhibit (2026-09-22, later)

* **Effective gradient, not text share.** Weighting each token by |adv|*(1-p) (the policy-gradient magnitude on the sampled logit), the
  curator rollout ranks 24th of 512 contributors to the step-34 update with 1.0 % of the effective weight (2.8 % of raw |adv|*tokens).
  85 % of its own effective weight sits on the 31k tokens before the loop (the /tmp/test3.py "hidden character" debugging spiral, 20 tool calls
  recreating and re-running one test file), 5 % on the 50k-token loop, 11 % on the exit and the fix. The earlier "8.3 % of positive-advantage
  reasoning text" was a character share and overstated its causal weight. The top step-34 contributors are ordinary rollouts (crawler-commons
  -3.61 x2, composer-unused +1.60 x2, graphql +2.46); the 134k-token pyccel runaway has 9.5 % of raw mass but 1.6 % effective.
* **Exit mechanics (token dumps).** The loop item is tokens [36,560, 86,717): 50,157 tokens, ~145 cycles of ~345 tokens (token-id autocorrelation
  74.8 % at lag 345). 99.9 % of its tokens have p > 0.99, mean (1-p) = 0.00026. It hardened over its first ~15k tokens (a few sampled tokens at
  p 0.4-0.8 once per cycle) and then ran ~35k tokens at p ~ 1. The exit was a single sampled token with p <= 3e-4 (logprob clipped at -8.0),
  after which `</think>` followed with p = 0.998 and the agent immediately ran the real test suite. The 2,000 tokens before the loop were already
  at mean lp -0.030 (normal text -0.13): a confidence ramp precedes entry. With exits at ~1e-4 per token, a hardened loop is expected to run to
  the context window; self-recovery is luck.
* **Two reinforcement channels, both older than step 34.** (a) Self-recovered loops with positive advantage: flex-937 x2 (step 21, 66k-token
  turns), curator (34, 50k), dot-prop-105 (35, 84k), runic.jl (36, 32k). (b) Runaways truncated at the context window that are still rewarded
  because the patch was already written before the loop (the harness evaluates the workspace; the loop is post-solution verification rumination):
  tfsec (20), dulwich-1073 (29), laravel (31), fastify-metrics, jinja-1782, jotai (all 36). In chain G 13 of 460 truncated rollouts scored 1
  (2.8 %); chain D 28/549, run B 12/226, run A 1/112, chain F 3/49. Channel (b) puts positive advantage on 120-150k-token loops (fastify +0.38,
  jinja +0.48, jotai +1.18 at step 36), although the loop bodies are gradient-inert; the entry regions are not.
* **"Let me try" did not rise after step 34** in ordinary reasoning: per million reasoning characters in non-loop rollouts it is 84 (32),
  109 (33), 137 (34), 76 (35). The curator-specific reinforcement story therefore does not show up in the next policy's text; the general
  blindness of the objective to loop bodies plus channel (b) remains the better explanation.
* **Channel (b) confirmed on all four step-36 cases** (sw-precache-244, fastify-metrics-61, jotai, jinja-1782): the last str_replace/create edit
  sits 60-230 items before the loop, "tests pass" reasoning appears before the loop and never after, the harness evaluates the workspace
  (resolved=True) and the loop is followed only by the harness message "Please continue working on the task ...". These are verification-phase
  ruminations after the task was solved, and they received +0.38 to +1.18 advantage over 80k-157k tokens.

steps 35-36, so it did not become more common after the step-34 update. About 60 % of chain G's loops contain "Given the time"; the rest cycle
on "I think", "But the test", "Let me check", "But that", "Actually," or a task-specific sentence.

## Feedback-loop analysis, steps 1-3 (analysis/loopfeedback, token-level, all arms, all steps; 2026-09-22)

Method: token dumps decoded with the base model's vocabulary; each `<think>` block scored (tokens, zlib ratio, distinct-8-gram ratio); classes
normal / loop-like (>=1000 tok, zlib<0.10, closed) / runaway (unclosed, truncated). For every sentence start the first 1/2/3 words, the sampling
probability of that token and the rollout advantage were aggregated per (arm, step, class, weight version). Scripts: lf_worker.py, report1-3b.py,
punct_worker.py, exit_worker.py under analysis/loopfeedback.

* Step 1 (labels): loop-like + runaway blocks hold 1-4 % of chain G's reasoning tokens at steps 1-6, 15-25 % at 24-34, 37-42 % at 35-36; run A
  <= 7 % and ~0 after step 20; chain F ~0; run B 2-15 %; chain D 3 % -> 38 %; chain I tracks chain G (23 % at step 8). zlib calibration: blocks
  with ratio < 0.10 are 76 % / 34 % / 13 % unclosed by bin, blocks >= 0.10 are <= 2.5 % unclosed.
* Candidate discovery (sentence starts in normal blocks, slope over all steps, run A and chain F as controls): grows in chain G but not in A/F:
  "i" (+2.1 per 1k per 10 steps vs -0.2/-1.2), "so" (+1.5 vs -2.5/-0.4), "i see", "i need to", "now i understand", "let me modify", "another/one
  approach is". Grows everywhere: "however", "alternatively", "we", "given". Grows in both MINF-engine arms G and F but not vLLM A: "but" (+11.4).
  Shrinks in G: "actually" (-2.3, flat/up in A/F), "wait", "this", "unless". "although" is essentially absent (0.01 per 1k) in all arms.
  Loop-enriched starts (share inside loops / share in normal): "i'm going" 40x, "but i'll" 32x, "i'll make/output/provide/stop/just/try/revert/
  write/submit" 11-20x, "i'm out" 16x, "i think" 11x, "given" 6.8x: loops are announced-but-unexecuted wrap-ups. Generic cycle sentences are
  objections: "but that's not possible" (13 rollouts), "but that's a big change", "but that didn't work", "but it already does that".
* Step 2+3 (push vs response): excess push(u,s) = signed fraction of u's gradient weight that is positive, relative to the batch. Across 469
  single-word units the rank correlation between mean excess push and usage slope is +0.02 in chain G (A -0.09, F 0.00, B -0.07, D -0.15);
  bigrams the same. The units that grow in chain G have NEGATIVE excess push ("given" -0.17, "given the" -0.19, "i'll try" -0.33, "i'll" -0.11):
  the reward signal on them points down while their usage rises. No phrase-level reinforcement feedback loop is visible in these data.
* Punctuation mechanism: `.\n\n` (20.8 % of sentence ends in normal blocks) is never followed by `</think>`; a bare `.` (56.8 %) is followed by
  `</think>` 19.5 % of the time and precedes 93.7 % of all closes. Inside loops both variants are sampled at p ~ 0.99 and a bare `.` leads to a
  close 0.1 % of the time; self-exits from chain G loops have median p(</think>) 0.51 vs 0.93 in normal blocks. In normal blocks chain G's
  close rate per sentence fell from 155 to ~115 per 1,000 by step 8 and stayed flat to step 36, while run A, chain F, run B and chain D all
  lengthened their normal blocks (close/1k halving, 7 -> 13-19 sentences per block): normal-block deliberation length and looping are separate
  axes; chain G's whole change is in the tail.
* How loops end (exit_worker over all 2,455 loop blocks + 25k baseline blocks): loop cycles are 250-500 tokens with 0.7-0.94 token-id
  autocorrelation; inside a loop both `.` and `.\n\n` are sampled at p >= 0.98 (paragraph breaks are 36-40 % of sentence ends vs 24 % in
  normal blocks). Every self-exit in every arm follows a bare `.` (90 %) or a backtick/colon/newline token (10 %), never `.\n\n`. In chain G
  31 % of self-exits are "fork" exits where that final token was sampled at p < 0.05 (baseline blocks: 1.2 %); the other 69 % are smooth: the
  final `.` had ordinary probability and `</think>` then followed at p ~ 0.98, i.e. the loop state drifted to a point where closing became the
  confident choice. Fork shares by arm: I 42 %, G 31 %, B 28 %, F 22 %, D 20 %, A 17 %. Runaway blocks are the loop itself in the MINF-lineage
  arms (median 86k tokens in G, 70k in D, 50k in B) but short in run A (5.9k) and chain F (2.4k), where truncation comes from a long
  conversation rather than a loop. Self-exits per step in chain G rise from 0-7 (steps 1-23) to 15-18 (steps 24, 31, 33, 35, 36).
